# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Two distinct audiences on one site:
1. **Hiring managers / recruiters** (primary) — senior operators evaluating a candidate for Strategy & Ops, Product Ops, GTM Ops, Revenue Ops, or Business Ops roles. They skim fast, cross-reference LinkedIn/resume, and are judging credibility and seniority signal as much as content.
2. **Freelance/coaching prospects** (secondary) — individuals looking for help with money coaching, travel planning, or life/professional project management. They need to trust a stranger with money or a big trip, so warmth and specificity matter more than corporate polish here.

Job to be done: convince audience 1 to take a call/interview; convince audience 2 to fill out an inquiry form.

## Product Purpose

A personal portfolio + freelance-services site for Morgan Pugh (Strategy & Operations leader, 9+ years, Elevance Health/Cigna background, active AI builder). Two-tier structure: a React SPA (career portfolio + consulting services toggle) plus three static sub-pages (money, travel, projects) that go deeper on each freelance offering. Success = a recruiter reads it and takes a call; a freelance prospect reads it and submits an inquiry.

## Brand Personality

**Warm & approachable** + **builder / technical edge**. Not stiff corporate-executive, not quirky/gimmicky. The voice should read as: a sharp operator who also ships things herself (built a company-wide chatbot, hand-coded an internal training site, is building this very site with AI tools) — competent and current, but personable enough that a stranger would trust her with their travel budget or wedding logistics.

## Anti-references

- **Generic AI-generated template** — tiny uppercase tracked eyebrows above every section, "01/02/03" numbered step scaffolding, repeated identical card grids. The current site leans on all three heavily across all five pages; explicitly called out by the user as something to move away from.
- **Gimmicky / cluttered** — competing floating widgets (the live theme-swapper + the navigation compass both bottom-corner, both animating), too much simultaneous motion (pulsing dots, bouncing badges, spinning icons, cycling text) competing for attention on first load. Reads as "demo" rather than "hire me."
- Corporate/forgettable jargon is a secondary concern but less important than the two above.

## Design Principles

1. **Every claim should survive a recruiter's cross-check.** Job titles, dates, and achievements on the site must match the resume/LinkedIn Morgan actually sends — mismatches read as carelessness, not creative positioning.
2. **Lead with the rarest story, not the safest one.** Morgan's most differentiated material (catching a systemic pricing bug nobody else saw, soloing an AI chatbot still used company-wide, running AI-based competitive intel on competitor 10-Ks) is generalist "category of one" material per the positioning framework she's been reading — it should be foregrounded, not buried in a bullet.
3. **One register per widget.** Decorative/experimental UI (theme swapper, nav compass) should not compete with the primary hiring-manager task above the fold; delight is earned after credibility is established, not before.
4. **Warmth lives in the writing, not just the palette.** The personal/About section and the freelance pages already do this well — extend that voice rather than defaulting to corporate register in the career sections.
5. **Show, don't just tell, on the consulting side.** Freelance prospects trust specifics (named trips, named tools, real numbers) over adjectives ("executive-level precision") — keep sharpening toward concrete proof.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No stated special requirements beyond standard WCAG AA — but low-opacity white-on-navy text (white/40, white/20 placeholders) currently falls short of AA contrast in several places and should be corrected as a baseline hygiene fix, not a nice-to-have.
