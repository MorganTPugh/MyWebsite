Two Weeks, Three Countries, and a Magical Proposal.

Colorful Porto rooftops, Douro Valley vineyards, fairy-tale Sintra castles, cliffside Algarve sunsets, and a cliffside restaurant in Granada overlooking a glowing Alhambra — where Ruben popped the question.

Zero Jet Lag, Thanks to One Goofy Travel Pillow.

Some people ease into an international trip. Ruben and I hit the ground sleeping — an inflatable travel pillow and a 5:40 PM flight got us seven straight hours of sleep before touchdown at Heathrow. If you can force yourself to sleep early on an overnight flight, it’s the ultimate zero-jet-lag hack.

From London, a short hop to Porto kicked off two weeks through vibrant coastal cities, rolling vineyards, regal palaces, and a cliffside proposal neither of us saw coming.

The Route

Two flights bookending an eight-stop loop through Portugal and Spain — in by Porto, out through Granada, London on both ends.

Porto & the Douro Valley

Porto greeted us with colorful, tightly-packed architecture built upward to shade its own streets — and dinner that doesn’t start before 8 PM. Our first night was Francesinhas Al Forno da Baixa: Ruben brave enough for the legendary meat-and-egg Francesinha sandwich, me safely in pizza territory, both of us finishing the night with gelato and street musicians drifting up to our balcony.

The next day was a full sweep — Torre dos Clérigos, the Ribeira waterfront, a walk across the Dom Luís I Bridge, and a sunset at Jardim do Morro that’s easily the best seat in the city. We recharged with an affogato at 7G Roasters and closed the night with garlic shrimp pasta and Port wine along the Douro River.

A train out to Pinhão took us through the Douro Valley — one of the most scenic rail routes in the world, all emerald vineyard terraces rolling down to the river. At Quinta do Jalloto, a 25-acre family-run estate, the owner walked us through the vines, olive trees, and beehives before sitting us down to charcuterie, honey, and rich local reds, with a curious wild fox trotting by for scraps.

Lisbon, Sintra & Cascais

We started in Lisbon at Mercado do Bolhão for fresh bread, cured ham, and juice, then took a day trip to Sintra — colorful palaces, manicured gardens, and a descent down the spiral stone staircase into the underground wells at Quinta da Regaleira. The tour wrapped in Cascais, cocktails in hand along the coast.

Back in the city, we spent a full day wandering Alfama — the historic, maze-like neighborhood of tiled facades and yellow streetcars squeaking up impossibly steep hills.

Lagos & the Algarve

A smooth, $10 bus ride south to Lagos stole our hearts almost immediately — incredible food, cliffside views, and some of the best coastline of the entire trip.

Ruben’s old scooter-driving skills paid off: we rented one for the day and cruised past farmland, hidden coves, and cliffside ruins, catching the sunset over the Atlantic before wandering into the town’s annual trade fair — food trucks, street performers, and vendors lining the water.

Seville, Granada & the Big Moment

A Cliffside Yes Above the Alhambra

Crossing into Spain, Seville’s heat hit immediately — vibrant, romantic, historic. We rented scooters to cross the Guadalquivir River, take in the scale of Plaza de España, and wander the shaded paths of Parque de María Luisa.

The next day, we drove through dramatic mountain landscapes to Granada, white-washed houses stacked against the Sierra Nevada. After touring the Alhambra — intricate Islamic geometry, courtyard fountains, centuries of history — the trip hit its peak that evening: Ruben proposed at a cliffside restaurant overlooking the illuminated Alhambra. Ancient palace glowing across the valley, most unforgettable moment of our lives.

We couldn’t stop staring at that view — so the next morning, we had breakfast on a terrace with the Alhambra right in front of us, ring and all.

A London Finale

Back in London for three final days, we celebrated the engagement in style: Big Ben, the Houses of Parliament, and the Thames by day; hidden speakeasies and live music through Soho and Covent Garden by night.

Fish and chips, pints, and football at a proper neighborhood pub. Pastry runs fueling every walk through the colorful alleys of Neal’s Yard — the perfect, high-energy finale to a trip that took us from the winding alleys of Porto to a cliffside proposal in Granada.
